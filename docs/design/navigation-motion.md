# Navigation motion

Research notes for swipe-driven page and drawer transitions. The timings below are starting points, not universal perceptual laws.

## What the evidence supports

- **Motion should explain the state change.** Apple recommends purposeful, brief motion that follows the user's gesture and expectations, can be cancelled, and does not make people wait. Its current guidance does not prescribe a universal duration. [Apple Human Interface Guidelines: Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)
- **Design-system durations are starting heuristics, not research-derived optima.** Material's older duration guidance uses 300 ms for typical transitions and longer durations for larger/full-screen movement, adjusted for travel distance and surface change. The current Material 3 motion system is spring-based and replaces fixed duration/easing slots for its motion scheme. Neither prescribes a measured optimum for this app. [Material: Duration & easing](https://m1.material.io/motion/duration-easing.html), [Material 3: Motion](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion)
- **For mobile page switches, response and readiness matter at least as much as animation length.** Ge et al. (2024) tested opening apps and switching in-app pages on a single Android phone. Their 60 participants rated fluency under different start delays (100/250/400 ms), animation durations (200/350/500 ms), and content-completion delays. Start delay and completion delay significantly affected fluency; animation duration alone was significant only in one opening-app condition. In general, shorter phases felt more fluent. This argues for starting movement promptly and making the destination useful quickly, rather than polishing a long transition while the UI waits. Limits: controlled tasks, a single device/OS, participants selected for high interest in animation, and self-reported fluency. [Ge et al., *International Journal of Human–Computer Studies* (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2024.103257)
- **Continuity can earn its time when it preserves spatial relationships.** Bederson and Boltman found that animated viewpoint changes helped users reconstruct a fixed spatial information layout without increasing task time. That supports directional continuity for navigation, but the study was about zoomable information spaces, not modern mobile tabs; it does not establish a drawer/page duration. [Bederson & Boltman, *Does Animation Help Users Build Mental Maps of Spatial Information?* (1999)](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/801854)
- **Touch feedback must begin quickly; this is not the same as a transition's total duration.** Kaaresoja et al. measured touchscreen button feedback: perceived quality dropped at longer touch-to-feedback delays, with visual feedback degrading notably between 100 and 150 ms. Use this as a warning against delayed first movement, not as proof that a page animation must finish within 100 ms. [Kaaresoja, Brewster & Lantz, *ACM Transactions on Applied Perception* (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1145/2611387)

## Rules for swipe navigation

1. **Make the gesture itself the animation.** During a recognized drag, position should track finger displacement and direction. A fixed-duration animation should only settle the remaining distance after release.
2. **Start visual feedback promptly.** Avoid waiting for route work or data fetches before showing the page movement. Keep first-frame latency and destination readiness separate from the easing curve; delayed or blank content feels like app latency even when the transition is smooth.
3. **Preserve reversibility until commit.** If the user releases short or reverses direction, return to the prior page/drawer state. Do not signal a completed navigation before the route state commits.
4. **Keep one owner for each motion.** If a native pager already moves its pages, do not add a second full-screen slide/fade to the same transition. The duplicated movement obscures the gesture-to-content mapping and can feel laggy.
5. **Use distance-aware release motion.** Prefer the platform pager/drawer's native settling behavior. For a custom noninteractive transition, use a consistent app motion scheme and tune it against the actual travel distance; the older 300 ms Material value is only a starting reference. Avoid making frequent navigation wait on a decorative tail.
6. **Keep direction and dismissal coherent.** A page entered from the right should leave toward the right when reversing; a drawer should follow its opening/closing edge and the finger. Avoid unrelated scale, bounce, or parallax unless it communicates hierarchy or state.
7. **Honor reduced motion without losing function.** Remove or simplify automatic visual travel while keeping the route result, direct user-driven swipes, cancellation behavior, and accessibility actions.

## How to tune this app

Keep native paging as the baseline and assess it on real iOS and Android devices. If its release settle feels wrong, compare a small set of short release curves (for example 200, 275, and 350 ms) while holding gesture thresholds constant. Observe: time from recognized movement to first visible pixel, whether the content stays under the finger, release-to-stable-state time, accidental commits/cancels, frame drops, and whether users can immediately continue interacting. The experiment should decide whether the change helps; a single duration copied from a guideline cannot.

For drawers, separately assess edge/threshold recognition and the visual follow-through. If the drawer is not directly tracking the user's finger, fix that interaction model before adjusting its easing or duration.
